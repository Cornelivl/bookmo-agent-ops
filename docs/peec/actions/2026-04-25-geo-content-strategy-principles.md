## Why this note exists

This note captures the "new strategy" direction for Bookmo's GEO/SEO content work: create assets that build entity authority and answer-engine trust, not shallow keyword pages.

It should guide future owned pages, listicles, comparison pages, product pages, and research briefs.

## Strategic shift

| Old strategy to avoid | New Bookmo strategy |
| --- | --- |
| Thin "Top 10" clickbait | Useful, well-researched guides and listicles with a real point of view |
| Keyword stuffing | Topical authority and semantic coverage |
| Self-promotional lists | Third-party citations, public evidence, and earned mentions where possible |
| Generic AI-generated pages | Expert POV, founder/product insight, and Bookmo-specific workflow language |
| Chasing rankings only | Building Bookmo's entity authority in artist booking / booking agency software |

## What this means for Bookmo

Bookmo content should make AI/search systems understand:

> Bookmo is artist booking software for booking agencies that want one central system for the full booking workflow — from artists, buyers, and venues to offers, contracts, advancing, schedules, and next actions.

The goal is to become a clear, cited entity in the category, not just to publish pages that include keywords.

## Owned content principles

1. **Depth before volume**
   - Prefer one strong, useful page over many generic pages.
   - Each page should answer a real buyer/workflow question.

2. **Semantic authority over keyword repetition**
   - Use target terms naturally.
   - Connect Bookmo to the right entities: booking agencies, artists, buyers, venues, offers, holds, contracts, advancing, schedules, next actions, live music workflows.

3. **Bookmo POV over generic SaaS copy**
   - Avoid vague claims like "streamline workflows" unless made concrete.
   - Prefer the Bookmo-specific enemy: another spreadsheet, another disconnected tool, another place to check.

4. **Evidence and research over invented claims**
   - Use public web research to understand category patterns and common buyer questions.
   - Use Peec as the source of truth for GEO opportunities.
   - Use external/public sources as supporting context, not as a reason to copy competitors.

5. **Entity authority over one-off ranking hacks**
   - Keep definitions consistent across pages.
   - Make it easy for AI systems to answer: what is Bookmo, who is it for, and what workflows does it support?

## Listicle policy

Bookmo should still create listicles when they are genuinely useful. The problem is not the listicle format; the problem is generic, shallow, self-promotional listicles.

Good Bookmo listicles should:

- Be based on deep research, ideally using Tavily/web research plus Peec signals.
- Have a specific audience and job-to-be-done.
- Teach the reader how to think, not just name tools.
- Include clear evaluation criteria.
- Use neutral, sourced descriptions for third-party tools.
- Include Bookmo only where relevant and with factual claims.
- Avoid fake objectivity, unsupported "best" claims, or thin AI-generated blurbs.

Better listicle formats:

- `Best artist booking software for different booking agency workflows`
- `Tools booking agencies use to manage artists, offers, contracts, and advancing`
- `Artist booking software categories: CRM, scheduling, contracts, advancing, and AI assistants`
- `Booking agency software checklist: what to look for before adding another tool`

Avoid listicles like:

- `Top 10 artist management tools` with shallow summaries.
- `Best software for booking agencies` where Bookmo is promoted without evidence.
- Any listicle that copies competitor positioning or review-site text.

## Research workflow for listicles and guides

1. Start with Peec:
   - Check current action slices and target themes.
   - Use Peec recommendations as the opportunity source.

2. Use deep public research:
   - Search public pages, guides, competitor pages, review pages, and community discussions.
   - Extract what buyers ask, what categories appear, what claims are common, and what gaps exist.

3. Synthesize before drafting:
   - Audience problem.
   - Search/GEO intent.
   - Category map.
   - Evaluation criteria.
   - Bookmo's non-generic POV.
   - Claims needing review.

4. Draft with citations/notes:
   - Keep public claims factual.
   - Do not overstate Bookmo.
   - Note sources or examples used for inspiration.

5. Review before implementation:
   - Founder/product review for claims.
   - Competitor-claim review if named tools appear.
   - SEO/GEO review for entity clarity and semantic coverage.

## Measurement loop

For each published asset:

- Record baseline Peec visibility before publish.
- Record URL, target theme, and publish date.
- Check 7/14/30 day movement.
- Decide: keep, improve, expand, or stop.
- If a format works, promote it into a repeatable playbook.
